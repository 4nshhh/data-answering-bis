FOREWORD

This Indian Standard (First Revision) was adopted by the Bureau of Indian Standards, after the draft finalized by the Apiary Industry Sectional Committee had been approved by the Food and Agriculture Division Council.

Beekeeping is practiced in India on modern lines for the last nearly 5 decades. The needs of the beekeepers for the beekeeping equipment are increasing day by day. In addition, it is necessary for them to possess implements required for colony inspection, which are to be taken along when one visits an apiary. It is needless to say, that without such implements the beekeeper will not be in a position to attend apiary work properly. A beekeeper’s kit can possess all implements and material required for day-to-day management, minor repairs, sampling, etc. There can be many items that can be included in the beekeeper’s kit depending upon the size of the apiary and type of apiary work involved. This kit shall be useful to the beekeepers for their bee management work, and to attend to minor repairs of bee boxes and frames. The kit shall also be useful to the field staff of extension organizations in checking beehives dimensions, inspection of colonies and guidance to the beekeepers and in beekeeping survey work. In this standard however, a set of minimum number of items is recommended which would be useful for a beekeeper for normal and routine apiary work.

The standard was first published in 1994. This revision is being carried out to incorporate the following changes:

- a) Change of title from Beeman’s Kit to Beekeeper’s Kit; b) Gum boots are discarded from components; and c) Use of fire retardant fabric for gloves and apron/overall.
For the purpose of deciding whether a particular requirement of this standard is complied with, the final value, observed or calculated, expressing the result of a test or analysis, shall be rounded off in accordance with IS 2 : 1960 ‘Rules for rounding off numerical values (revised)’. The number of significant places retained in the rounded off value should be the same as that of the specified value in this standard.

## 3 COMPONENTS

The components are listed under two heads, namely implements and consumables. The latter shall have to be replenished as and when used.

### 3.1 Implements

The following sizes and specifications of the components shall be used.


#### 3.1.1 Hive Tool

It shall be a small flat metal piece of 20 cm length and 3 cm width and 0.3 cm thickness. Its one end shall be flat and sharpened and the other end shall also be sharpened but bent at 90°. There shall be a small hole to pull nails, etc, near the flat end of the hive tool. This tool is useful to loosen and take out the combs from the chamber when they are jammed due to propolis deposits and also to clean the hive parts like bottom board, frames/combs, etc. It can also be used for fixing small nails. Thus, it serves the purpose of a small hammer also (see Fig. 1).


#### 3.1.2 Bee Veil

Bee veil is similar to swarm net except at the top where the veil shall have a cap such that the cap fits on the head as and when used by the beekeeper (see Fig. 2). The colour of the net shall be black.



**FIG. 1 HIVE TOOL**


**FIG. 2 BEE VEIL**

#### 3.1.3 Gloves

A pair of gloves made of fire retardant fabric up to elbow shall be used. These are useful to protect hands at the time of colony inspection especially if the colony is ferocious and stinging. Elbow end shall have elastic string to make it bee-tight.


#### 3.1.4 Apron or Overall

Apron/overall made of fire retardant fabric of preferably light colour (other than red) without any mosaics shall be used for covering whole of the body to avoid stinging.


#### 3.1.5 Smoker

The smoker shall be cylindrical in shape and made up



of zinc sheet (24G) and/or galvanized iron. It shall have a smoke outlet (nose cone) at one end. To it a bellow blower is attached, which when pressed, passes air into the smoker through the connection provided. The fuel inside the smoker emits smoke when suitably lit with fire. The cylindrical body of smoker should preferably be double chambered with the inner chamber to be used as fire box in order to prevent heating of outer surface of smoker to avoid burning injuries. Additional protection using wire screen around the cylindrical body of the smoker may also be provided. The smoker can be opened from the rear end. Few puffs of smoke are used to tranquilize the bees which facilitates inspection of colonies (see Fig. 3).


**FIG. 3 SMOKER**


#### 3.1.6 Bee Brush

It shall be single row hair brush having light soft bristles about 5 cm long (see Fig. 4). It is used to brush off bees from the combs and from the hive parts either during inspection or for removing the bees from honey combs prior to honey extraction.


**FIG. 4 BEE BRUSH**


#### 3.1.7 Wire Embedder

It is a small tool used to fix comb foundation in the empty wooden frame onto the frame wires.

### 3.2 Consumables


#### 3.2.1 Brood and Super Frames

The dimensions for brood and super frames of Type A,



B and C beehives shall be as per IS 1515 depending on the region.


#### 3.2.2 Comb Foundation Sheets

Few beeswax made comb foundation sheets conforming to IS 2072 shall be used (see Fig. 13).


**FIG. 13 COMB FOUNDATION SHEETS**


#### 3.2.3 Queen Gate

The queen gate shall be of suitable passage size depending upon the area.


#### 3.2.4 Queen Cage

The queen cage shall be made of piece of wood with three semi circular holes on top side (Benton type) and covered with wire gauze. It shall have one circular hole towards one of the sides of the cage which shall open in one of the semi-circular hole in the front of the cage. All these three semi-circular holes shall be connected in row through a shallow groove. It is useful for transporting queen bee from one place to another or for introduction of queen bee in a queenless colony.


#### 3.2.5 Thread

Ordinary gunny and cotton thread shall be used for stitching. This is useful for tying broken combs in the frames and for any other miscellaneous work.


#### 3.2.6 Candle, Match Box and Beeswax

These are useful for fixing comb foundations in the frames.



#### 3.2.7 Empty Vials, Paper Envelops and Bottles

Properly cleaned empty vials and bottles with rubber stoppers and small paper envelops shall be used. They are useful for taking samples of honey, pollen, honey bees, etc, from the apiary.


#### 3.2.8 Adhesive Bandage, Antiseptic Liquid and Cotton

Adhesive bandage, antiseptic liquid and cotton shall be used for first aid.


#### 3.2.9 Anti Histamine Cream

Anti histamine cream should be used for smearing on body parts where bees have stung.
