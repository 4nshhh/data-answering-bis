FOREWORD

This Indian Standard (Fourth Revision) was adopted by the Bureau of Indian Standards, after the draft finalized by the Apiary Industry Sectional Committee had been approved by the Food and Agriculture Division Council.

From the very beginning of beekeeping in movable frame hives, various types of beehives have been used in different parts of the country. Such practice of using different types of hives created problems to the beekeeper in efficient management of colonies. In order to remove these difficulties, this standard was first published in 1959 and was revised in 1969, 1981 and 1998.

Until second revision (1981), the standard provided provision for only two types of beehives, namely Type A and Type B for beekeeping of native bees, primarily A. cerana indica and  A. cerana cerana.

With the beekeeping of A. mellifera becoming popular in the country and on account of different ecosystems, bees of larger sizes prevalent within the country, need was felt for third type of beehives. The third revision was carried out to incorporate the requirements for third beehive type designated as Type C. The Type C is based on ‘Langstroth’ type and has been modified to suit the Indian conditions.

This revision has been carried out to incorporate the following changes:

- a) Modification in terminology of brood frame (comb), dummy board and floor board.
- b) Use of plastic in making of beehives has been discarded.
- c) Dimension of entrance gate has been modified.
- d) The position of frame to the entrance in brood chamber and super chamber (Exploded assembly) has been modified lengthwise as against breadth wise in the third revision.
For the purpose of deciding whether a particular requirement of this standard is complied with, the final value, observed or calculated, expressing the result of a test or analysis, shall be rounded off in accordance with IS 2 : 1960 ‘Rules for rounding off numerical values (revised)’. The number of significant places retained in the rounded off value should be the same as that of the specified value in this standard.

## 3 TERMINOLOGY

For the purpose of this standard, the following definitions shall apply.


### 3.1 Bee Space — The optimum space between two combs in a beehive essential for the normal movement and functioning of bees in combs.


3.2 Brood Chamber — A four-sided wooden box of rectangular cross-section without a top and a bottom, in which the combs/frames (see 3.3) are placed.


3.3 Frame — A frame of wood, of suitable size, to hold a comb. It is made up of a top bar, a bottom bar and two side bars in which the bees raise comb to rear brood and the comb is a fully raised frame by the bees through elaboration of bees wax from their own body. The frames are so constructed that a series of them may be placed in a vertical position in the brood chamber or the super chamber so as to provide free bee space in between them for bees to move.


### 3.4 Inner Cover — A partition between the brood chamber/super chamber and the top cover.


### 3.5 Dummy Board — A wooden board deeper and


wider than the frame and placed in the brood chamber/ super chamber to prevent the bees from going beyond it, thus limiting the capacity of the brood chamber. This is also used in parallel at the end of combs, generally, in weak and developing colonies.


3.6 Entrance Rod — A wooden block at the hive entrance usually provided with slot of suitable size for bees to exit and return into hive after foraging. The slot may be covered with queen excluder sheet (see 3.12).


3.7 Floor (Bottom) Board — A wooden board on which the brood chamber rests. It is longer in size than the brood chamber.


### 3.8 Reinforcement — A wire parallel to the top bar and attached to the side bars of frames to reinforce the comb.


3.9 Super Chamber — A four-sided wooden box of rectangular cross-section without top and bottom, which is placed on the brood chamber when the colony is growing and need more space either for brood rearing or for honey storage, in which the combs/frames (see 3.3) are placed. It is similar to brood chamber (see 3.2) but of lesser height for Type A and Type B hives and of same height in Type C hive and placed above it when required.


### 3.10 Super Frame — A frame (see 3.3) which has a depth less than that of the brood frame (see 3.2) for Type A and Type B hives but the same as brood frame in Type C hive.


3.11 Top Cover/Roof — A wooden cover with four hanging sides and a top and placed on the inner cover. It is a cover of bee hive lined on top and sides with zinc coated metal sheet/aluminium for protection against rains.


3.12 Queen Excluder — A set of openings meant for restricting the passing of queen bee. It is placed in between brood chamber and honey/super chamber during honey flow.


3.13 Queen Gate — A provision made at the entrance by a piece of queen excluder (see 3.12) for preventing queen from going out of hive and enemies like wasps, etc, which are bigger than worker bees, from entering into hive.

## 4 TYPES AND SIZES OF BEEHIVES
